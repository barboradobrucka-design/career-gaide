# career-gaide
Building AI course project

## Summary
The tool is designed for students seeking skills and knowledge required in today's job market. Its aim is to help users prepare effectively, since school systems often lack flexibility and do not always match employers' expectations. 

## Background
Career gAIde is an AI assistant for students that offers learning recommendations that match current job market requirements. The tool generates personalized learning paths based on each student’s specific career interests. The goal is to address deficiencies within school system and equip students with the skills necessary for successful entry into the workforce.

## How is it used?
Career gAIde works as a personal assistant available as a web or mobile application. After users provide their university field of study, desired job role, and timeframe for learning, the application compares skills acquired at school with those currently expected in the job market and suggests an individual learning path.

## Data sources and AI methods
The data source for this project will be LinkedIn as well as other big platforms that advertise open positions including local job market platforms specific to a country where this tool will be implemented. Local companies that partner with universities are another possible source. Content based filtering, deep learning models, reinforcement learning are AI techniques that will be used for skills and knowledge recommendations. Just like on Netflix, YouTube or Spotify.

## Challenges
Career gAIde does not guarantee higher-paying positions or immediate employment for graduates. However, it can enhance their confidence and provide insight into the skills and knowledge employers seek, as well as how these requirements evolve over time.
This tool may become overwhelming and confusing for students due to the countless expectations associated with similar positions. As a result, it can suggest more tasks than students are able to manage while studying. To address this issue, implementing limitations during the creation of learning path—such as picking most common skill requirements, setting 3–5 study hours per week or focusing on one skill per quarter—could help address this issue. 
Forming partnerships with numerous companies can be difficult and time-consuming for both schools and businesses. 
Another challenge may involve the technical aspect, as it will require not only integrating existing job market platform demands but also incorporating manual input from companies planning to create positions that do not yet exist. This may present challenges, especially in regions that are sparsely populated and less likely to attract new residents.

## What next?
This project, at the end of the day, might serve as valuable source of information for universities and high schools allowing them to adjust their teaching portfolio and keep up with labor market expectations. Career gAIde benefits local companies by preparing students to adapt quickly once hired.
Another potential application for this recommendation system is developing a similar tool for high school students to assist them in selecting universities. This tool could consider factors such as academic programs, tuition fees, and graduate employment outcomes.
